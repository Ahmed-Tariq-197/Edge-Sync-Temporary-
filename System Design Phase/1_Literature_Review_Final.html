THEME 1

Theme 1 — Edge-Friendly Tracking Backbone

What the literature says: The detector/tracker layer is a supporting technology, not the project novelty. The literature converges on real-time online tracking, with camera-motion compensation becoming important when the camera itself moves. The main disagreement is accuracy versus edge cost.
PAPER 1
Redmon, Divvala, Girshick & Farhadi 2016
1. Problem Statement
Provide real-time object detection fast enough for constrained edge hardware.
2. Methodology & Algorithms
YOLO — single regression network predicting boxes, confidence and class per grid cell in one pass, replacing two-stage region-proposal pipelines.
3. Datasets
PASCAL VOC (+ People-Art, Picasso for generalization)
4. Evaluation Metrics
mAP, FPS, localization error, false-positive rate (45 FPS base / ~155 FPS Fast-YOLO)
5. Key Results (SOTA)
Real-time, end-to-end, simple, suited to constrained edge hardware.
6. Limitations
Lower localization accuracy than two-stage detectors; weak on small objects; detection errors propagate downstream.
7. Research Gap / Relevance to the Proposed Problem
Relevance: Real-time, end-to-end, simple, suited to constrained edge hardware. Constraint to address in the proposed work: Lower localization accuracy than two-stage detectors; weak on small objects; detection errors propagate downstream.
PAPER 2
Wojke, Bewley & Paulus (DeepSORT) ICIP 2017
1. Problem Statement
Preserve target identity across visual changes and/or camera views.
2. Methodology & Algorithms
SORT + a CNN appearance embedding per detection; Mahalanobis motion gate fused with cosine appearance distance via Hungarian assignment.
3. Datasets
MOT16 (embedding trained on person Re-ID data)
4. Evaluation Metrics
MOTA, ID switches, fragmentation (~45% fewer ID switches than SORT)
5. Key Results (SOTA)
Names the exact mechanism (appearance embedding) the perception component needs; lightweight; embeddings reusable for Re-ID.
6. Limitations
Single-camera design; fixed linear motion model; appearance drifts under illumination/viewpoint change.
7. Research Gap / Relevance to the Proposed Problem
Relevance: Names the exact mechanism (appearance embedding) the perception component needs; lightweight; embeddings reusable for Re-ID. Constraint to address in the proposed work: Single-camera design; fixed linear motion model; appearance drifts under illumination/viewpoint change.
PAPER 3
Zhang et al. (ByteTrack) 2021–22
1. Problem Statement
Preserve target identity across visual changes and/or camera views.
2. Methodology & Algorithms
Associates both high- and low-confidence detection boxes to existing tracks, recovering targets under occlusion/blur without a Re-ID branch.
3. Datasets
MOT17, MOT20, BDD100K
4. Evaluation Metrics
MOTA 80.3, HOTA 63.1, IDF1, >30 FPS
5. Key Results (SOTA)
Fast, simple, recovers low-score boxes; strong real-time baseline.
6. Limitations
Degrades under non-linear camera motion without GMC; single-camera only; no identity transfer across views.
7. Research Gap / Relevance to the Proposed Problem
Relevance: Fast, simple, recovers low-score boxes; strong real-time baseline. Constraint to address in the proposed work: Degrades under non-linear camera motion without GMC; single-camera only; no identity transfer across views.
PAPER 4
Cao, Maggiolino, Ahmad & Kitani (OC-SORT / Deep OC-SORT) 2023
1. Problem Statement
Preserve target identity across visual changes and/or camera views.
2. Methodology & Algorithms
Observation-Centric Re-Update & Momentum removes Kalman velocity-accumulation error; Deep variant only blends appearance once a confidence check passes.
3. Datasets
MOT17, MOT20, DanceTrack, KITTI
4. Evaluation Metrics
HOTA 63.2, MOTA 78.5, IDF1 (SOTA among real-time trackers at publication)
5. Key Results (SOTA)
Robust to non-linear motion and occlusion-corrupted embeddings; adaptive Re-ID fusion beats fixed-threshold cosine matching.
6. Limitations
Still single-session; ID swaps possible after >30 missed frames; more compute than plain DeepSORT.
7. Research Gap / Relevance to the Proposed Problem
Relevance: Robust to non-linear motion and occlusion-corrupted embeddings; adaptive Re-ID fusion beats fixed-threshold cosine matching. Constraint to address in the proposed work: Still single-session; ID swaps possible after >30 missed frames; more compute than plain DeepSORT.
PAPER 5
Du, Zhao, Song, Zhao, Su, Gong & Meng (StrongSORT) IEEE TMM 2023
1. Problem Statement
Improve online multi-object tracking accuracy and identity continuity through lightweight upgrades to a DeepSORT-style pipeline.
2. Methodology & Algorithms
Overhauls DeepSORT's sub-components: BoT features, ECC camera-motion compensation, NSA Kalman filter, EMA updating; adds AFLink and GSI plug-ins.
3. Datasets
MOT17, MOT20
4. Evaluation Metrics
New HOTA/IDF1 records; StrongSORT++ +2.1 IDF1/AssA over prior SOTA
5. Key Results (SOTA)
Direct, modern, lightweight upgrade path for a DeepSORT-style pipeline; plug-ins are edge-cheap.
6. Limitations
Still single-camera; GSI interpolation is offline, hindering pure real-time use; CMC adds overhead.
7. Research Gap / Relevance to the Proposed Problem
Relevance: Direct, modern, lightweight upgrade path for a DeepSORT-style pipeline; plug-ins are edge-cheap. Constraint to address in the proposed work: Still single-camera; GSI interpolation is offline, hindering pure real-time use; CMC adds overhead.

THEME 2

Theme 2 — Cross-Camera / Cross-Agent Identity Transfer

What the literature says: The central issue is identity continuity when the target leaves one view and appears in another. Appearance is useful but fragile under viewpoint and occlusion; the strongest direction is combining appearance with spatial and temporal evidence. Mobile-robot viewpoint shift remains underrepresented.

Theme 4 — Proactive Handoff, Occlusion Forecasting & Time-to-Event Prediction

What the literature says: This is the closest theme to the proposed research gap. Prior work proves proactive trackability-based handoff and lightweight time-to-event estimation, but there is still a mismatch between those ideas and physical moving-UGV target handoff with Re-ID.

SYNTHESIS

What the Literature Agrees On — and Where It Disagrees

Strong consensus

  • Online tracking must balance identity continuity with compute cost; for moving cameras, camera-motion compensation is important.
  • Appearance-only Re-ID is insufficient under viewpoint change and occlusion; spatial/temporal evidence should assist identity decisions.
  • Raw video exchange is a poor fit for decentralized edge systems; compact target state and embedding metadata are preferable.
  • Proactive handoff is superior in principle to waiting for target loss, because handoff requires lead time for communication, decision, and receiver preparation.
  • Lightweight kinematic prediction is a credible edge baseline; learned predictors are useful but add deployment risk and must justify their compute cost.
  • Distributed allocation/auction methods are mature, but general task-allocation protocols do not automatically satisfy a sub-second handoff deadline.
  • Physical validation materially changes the engineering constraints; real robot experiments expose latency, localization, and communication issues that simulation can hide.

Main disagreements / trade-offs

  • Tracker choice: BoT-SORT/StrongSORT/BoostTrack++ each trade compute, appearance quality and robustness differently. No single benchmark proves one is universally best on edge UGVs.
  • Prediction complexity: learned trajectory models can improve non-linear prediction, while Kalman/kinematic models offer deterministic, cheap inference. The right answer is an empirical edge comparison.
  • Negotiation: auctions and consensus can provide stronger global assignment properties, but extra rounds increase deadline risk. A handoff event may justify a simpler one-round protocol.
  • Camera overlap: many multi-camera studies assume overlapping views; sparse UGV deployments cannot rely on continuous overlap and therefore need prediction + receiver pre-positioning.
  • Physical deployment: some “edge” papers still offload expensive perception to a server/laptop. That is useful feasibility evidence but does not satisfy a fully decentralized on-robot architecture.
FINAL SYNTHESIS

Final Research Gap Established by the Literature

What the literature establishes: The six themes converge on a consistent boundary. Modern online tracking can provide strong single-camera association, Re-ID can support identity continuity across non-overlapping views, edge systems can exchange compact metadata instead of video, and decentralized allocation can coordinate multiple agents. However, the literature reviewed here does not provide a single, physically validated UGV-to-UGV system that jointly addresses proactive prediction of visibility loss, explicit time-to-occlusion / time-to-event reasoning, latency-critical decentralized handoff, and identity-preserving transfer between moving robots.

Research gap: The unresolved problem is therefore not the invention of another detector, tracker, Re-ID network, or generic auction algorithm. It is the experimentally validated integration of predictive handoff timing, lightweight edge computation, discrete decentralized coordination, and cross-robot identity continuity under the constraints of moving physical UGVs.
