1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document defines the functional and non-functional requirements for a decentralized cooperative target-tracking and handoff system composed of multiple Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs).

The system shall enable a UGV that currently owns a target track to proactively transfer tracking responsibility to another UGV before the target becomes unavailable, while preserving target identity and operating under edge-computing and communication constraints.

This SRS defines what the system shall do. Specific algorithms, mathematical formulations, model selections, middleware choices, and implementation details shall be finalized in the subsequent System Design / Technical Design Report, bounded by the hardware constraints established in this version.

Hardware grounding: The baseline explicitly targets the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano as the physical edge platform. Exact runtime limits, power mode, thermal behavior, and application-level performance shall be established by Tier 2 benchmarking rather than assumed from vendor peak specifications.

2. System Motivation

The completed Literature Review established that the principal technologies required by the proposed system have substantial prior work: real-time detection and tracking, appearance-based Re-ID, cross-camera identity transfer, edge perception, compact metadata communication, proactive handoff, trajectory and time-to-event prediction, decentralized coordination, and physical multi-robot tracking.

The identified research opportunity is therefore not the invention of an isolated detector, tracker, or Re-ID model. The central problem is the integration of predictive timing, identity continuity, deadline-aware handoff feasibility, lightweight edge computation, discrete decentralized coordination, and physical validation on moving UGVs.

Core research problem: determine whether a target handoff can be completed successfully before the current tracking agent loses the target, while preserving target identity and respecting real-world timing and communication constraints.

3. Scope

3.1 In Scope

  • Onboard visual perception.
  • Target detection and multi-object tracking support.
  • Target state estimation.
  • Short-horizon target motion prediction.
  • Visibility-loss and Time-to-Occlusion estimation.
  • Proactive handoff triggering.
  • Handoff feasibility assessment.
  • Decentralized robot-to-robot coordination.
  • Receiver selection and handoff negotiation.
  • Cross-robot target identity transfer.
  • Target acquisition by the receiving UGV.
  • Re-identification and identity verification.
  • Handoff confirmation and ownership transfer.
  • Failure detection and recovery.
  • Logging and performance measurement.
  • Physical validation on moving UGVs.

3.2 Out of Scope

  • Development of a novel object detector from first principles.
  • Development of a novel Re-ID architecture from first principles.
  • General-purpose swarm-scale task allocation.
  • Centralized cloud perception as the primary operating mode.
  • Continuous raw-video exchange as the primary cooperation mechanism.
  • Guaranteeing performance across arbitrary environments and conditions.
  • Simulation-only validation as the final evidence of system effectiveness.

4. System Context

The primary validation scenario contains at least two UGVs. Robot A currently owns the target track; Robot B is a candidate receiving robot.

Target→Robot A
Current Tracking Owner→Handoff Decision→Handoff Metadata→Robot B
Receiving Agent→Acquisition + Re-ID→Confirmed Tracking

4.1 Robot A — Current Tracking Agent

Robot A is responsible for the active target track and initiates handoff when continued tracking is predicted to become unreliable or unavailable.

4.2 Robot B — Candidate Receiving Agent

Robot B evaluates whether it can acquire and verify the target within the available handoff time.

7. Functional Requirements

7.1 Visual Perception

FR-01 — Frame Acquisition. Each UGV shall acquire timestamped visual frames from its onboard camera.
FR-02 — Target Detection. The system shall detect target candidates and produce, at minimum, a bounding box, confidence value, class/category information, and timestamp.
FR-03 — Target Tracking. The system shall associate detections across consecutive frames and maintain a target track while sufficient visual evidence is available.
FR-04 — Tracking Confidence. The system shall maintain a confidence representation for the current target track and make it available to downstream decision functions.

7.2 Target State

FR-05 — Target State Representation. The system shall maintain a state representation containing, at minimum, target position, velocity, timestamp, and confidence. Additional state variables may be defined by the Technical Design Report.
FR-06 — Temporal Consistency. The system shall maintain a temporally ordered short history of target observations sufficient to support state estimation and prediction.

7.3 Prediction

FR-07 — Short-Horizon Prediction. The system shall estimate the target's future motion over a short prediction horizon appropriate to the handoff scenario.
FR-08 — Prediction Uncertainty. The system shall provide a representation of prediction uncertainty suitable for downstream handoff decision making.
FR-09 — Edge-Aware Prediction. The prediction function shall be deployable within the computational constraints of the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano edge platform.

7.4 Visibility and Time-to-Occlusion

FR-10 — Visibility Boundary Estimation. The system shall identify a visibility-loss condition under which the current UGV is expected to lose reliable visual tracking of the target, including physical occlusion and target exit from the effective camera field of view (FOV).
FR-11 — Time-to-Occlusion (TTO) Estimation. The system shall estimate the expected remaining time before the target reaches the defined visibility-loss boundary. For this project, TTO is an operational time-to-visibility-loss quantity and shall include both physical occlusion and target exit from the effective camera FOV when either condition defines impending loss of reliable tracking.
FR-12 — TTO Update. TTO shall be updated as target state, robot motion, visibility, and prediction information change.
FR-13 — Uncertainty-Aware TTO. The system shall support representation of uncertainty in the TTO estimate.

7.5 Handoff Decision

FR-14 — Proactive Handoff Trigger. Robot A shall be able to create a handoff candidate event before complete target loss.
FR-15 — Deadline Awareness. Handoff decisions shall consider the available time before target unavailability.
FR-16 — Handoff Feasibility. The system shall determine whether the expected handoff process can be completed within the available time budget, considering communication, decision, receiver motion constraints (position, velocity, navigation availability), target acquisition, identity verification, and safety requirements as applicable. The feasibility decision shall explicitly account for predicted TTO and expected end-to-end handoff completion time, including a defined safety margin.
FR-17 — Handoff Cancellation. Robot A shall be able to cancel or invalidate a pending handoff when its assumptions become invalid, including target trajectory change, receiver unavailability, communication timeout, or restored tracking conditions.

7.6 Receiving Robot

FR-18 — Receiver Discovery. The system shall identify or maintain knowledge of eligible neighboring UGVs that may act as receiving candidates.
FR-19 — Receiver State. A candidate receiver shall expose sufficient state information to evaluate its handoff capability, including availability and relevant acquisition/communication status.
FR-20 — Receiver Selection. The primary two-UGV validation scenario shall identify Robot B as the designated candidate receiver. The architecture shall nevertheless represent receiver eligibility and selection explicitly so that additional UGVs can be introduced later without changing ownership-transfer semantics.

7.7 Decentralized Negotiation

FR-21 — Peer-to-Peer Coordination. Core handoff coordination shall operate between UGV agents without requiring a central decision maker.
FR-22 — Handoff Request. Robot A shall transmit a handoff request containing sufficient metadata for Robot B to evaluate the request.
FR-23 — Receiver Response. Robot B shall return an explicit response indicating acceptance, rejection, unavailability, or timeout/failure.
FR-24 — Deadline-Constrained Negotiation. Negotiation shall be bounded so that coordination overhead does not consume the handoff time budget. In the primary two-UGV protocol, the handoff transaction shall use a bounded request/response exchange and shall not enter an unbounded negotiation loop.
FR-25 — Event-Triggered Communication. Handoff communication shall be triggered by meaningful state/events rather than requiring continuous negotiation traffic.

7.8 Communication

FR-26 — Metadata-Based Communication. The primary inter-robot cooperation mechanism shall use compact target and handoff metadata rather than continuous raw-video streaming.
FR-27 — Message Timestamping. Every handoff-related message shall contain a timestamp.
FR-28 — Sender Identification. Every inter-robot message shall identify its sending robot.
FR-29 — Target Identification. Handoff messages shall contain information sufficient to associate the message with the intended target.
FR-30 — Message Validity. The receiving agent shall detect stale, duplicated, malformed, or unexpected handoff messages.
FR-31 — Communication Failure. Temporary packet loss, delay, disconnection, or receiver timeout shall not be interpreted as successful ownership transfer.

7.9 Identity and Re-Identification

FR-32 — Identity Representation. The system shall maintain an identity representation that permits target comparison across observations from different UGVs.
FR-33 — Cross-Robot Re-ID. Robot B shall verify whether the acquired target corresponds to the target previously tracked by Robot A.
FR-34 — Multi-Cue Identity Verification. The design shall permit identity verification to use appearance together with other available evidence such as spatial, temporal, or predicted-state consistency.
FR-35 — Re-ID Rejection. Robot B shall be able to reject acquisition when identity confidence does not satisfy the defined acceptance condition.

7.10 Target Acquisition

FR-36 — Receiver Acquisition. Following an accepted handoff, Robot B shall attempt to acquire the target using its onboard perception.
FR-37 — Acquisition Timeout. Target acquisition shall be subject to a bounded acquisition interval.
FR-38 — Acquisition Verification. Visual detection alone shall not constitute successful ownership transfer; acquisition shall be followed by identity verification and track establishment.

7.11 Handoff Confirmation and Ownership

FR-39 — Handoff Confirmation. The system shall declare handoff successful only after target acquisition, identity verification, track establishment, and ownership-transfer conditions are satisfied.
FR-40 — Ownership Transfer. After successful confirmation, Robot B shall become the active tracking owner of the target.
FR-41 — Previous Owner State. Robot A shall transition out of exclusive target ownership after confirmed handoff and enter the state defined for post-handoff operation.

7.12 Failure and Recovery

FR-42 — Failed Negotiation. Failed negotiation shall not result in ownership transfer.
FR-43 — Failed Acquisition. Failure to acquire the target within the allowed interval shall invalidate the handoff unless a defined recovery condition is met.
FR-44 — Re-ID Failure. Failed identity verification shall invalidate the ownership transfer.
FR-45 — Communication Failure. Communication failure during handoff shall cause the system to enter an appropriate recovery state.
FR-46 — Stale State Protection. The system shall detect and avoid unqualified use of stale target or receiver state.
FR-47 — Duplicate Ownership Prevention. The system shall prevent an inconsistent state in which multiple UGVs simultaneously claim exclusive ownership of the same target without a defined transitional handoff state.

7.13 State Management

FR-48 — Handoff State Machine. The system shall maintain explicit handoff states covering tracking, candidate generation, negotiation, commitment, acquisition, identity verification, confirmation, and failure/recovery.
TRACKING→HANDOFF CANDIDATE→NEGOTIATING→COMMITTED→ACQUISITION→IDENTITY VERIFICATION→CONFIRMED

FAILED / TIMEOUT / INVALID→RECOVERY→TRACKING OR TERMINAL FAILURE
Important: Commitment does not constitute ownership transfer. Ownership changes only after successful acquisition, identity verification, track establishment, and explicit handoff confirmation.

7.14 Physical Robot Operation

FR-49 — Onboard Perception. Core perception functions shall be deployable on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano edge computing platform.
FR-50 — Moving-Robot Operation. The system shall support target tracking while the UGV carrying the active camera is moving.
FR-51 — Robot-to-Robot Handoff. The validated handoff scenario shall represent transfer of target tracking ownership between physical UGVs.
FR-52 — Physical Validation. The integrated handoff process shall be evaluated on physical moving UGVs as the mandatory primary validation tier. Simulation and edge benchmarking shall support development and diagnosis but shall not replace the physical validation requirement.

7.15 Cross-Robot Consistency and Liveness

FR-53 — Shared Reference Frame. All target state exchanged between UGVs shall be expressed in an explicitly identified shared reference frame (frame identifier carried in every message that contains spatial data), together with the sender's pose uncertainty. The receiver shall inflate the transferred target uncertainty by the combined sender/receiver localization uncertainty before using it for acquisition or identity gating.
FR-54 — Confirmation Acknowledgement and Ownership Epoch. Handoff confirmation shall be acknowledged by the previous owner. Every ownership-related message shall carry a monotonically increasing ownership epoch; messages carrying a stale epoch shall be rejected. The previous owner shall retain exclusive tracking responsibility until the acknowledgement exchange completes. If the confirmation acknowledgement is lost, the receiver shall abort after a timeout. The owner shall transition to STANDBY only upon receiving a reliably delivered ownership claim from the receiver (role OWNER, epoch e+1, matching transaction). It shall reclaim the target at a strictly higher epoch (e+2) immediately upon receiving HANDOFF_CANCEL for that transaction, or after a bounded timeout T_claim measured from the instant the acknowledgement was sent. A robot holding the OWNER role shall demote itself on receiving an ownership claim with a higher epoch. The guarantee is at most one OWNER per epoch, with divergence bounded by T_claim and automatic convergence to the highest epoch.
FR-55 — Liveness Monitoring. While a handoff is in the COMMITTED, ACQUISITION, or IDENTITY_VERIFICATION states, the receiver shall emit periodic liveness/status messages and the owner shall monitor them. Loss of liveness beyond a configured bound shall abort the transaction and trigger recovery without waiting for the full transaction timeout (T_txn).
FR-56 — Bounded Failover. In the two-UGV baseline, a failed or aborted handoff shall enter RECOVERY (continued best-effort tracking by the owner). Re-attempting with another candidate shall be permitted only when another eligible candidate exists and the remaining TTO still exceeds the end-to-end handoff budget.

9.1 Required Message Properties

  • Sender identity.
  • Target association.
  • Transaction identity separate from target identity.
  • Timestamp.
  • Message/event type.
  • Required target state or handoff metadata.
  • Validity/freshness information where required.
  • Protocol/state information sufficient to reject stale or out-of-sequence messages.

9.2 Ownership and Transaction Semantics

Ownership invariant: A handoff transaction, acceptance/commit message, or communication acknowledgment shall never by itself constitute ownership transfer. Ownership shall change only after receiver acquisition, identity verification, track establishment, and explicit handoff confirmation. target_id identifies the tracked entity; transaction_id identifies the specific handoff attempt.

The exact message schema and transport protocol are design decisions to be specified in the Technical Design Report.

12. System-Level Success Criteria

Primary success criterion: The integrated system shall demonstrate, on physical moving UGVs, that a tracking UGV can predict impending target unavailability, determine whether a receiver can complete the handoff within the available time and safety margin, coordinate the transfer without centralized control, enable the receiver to acquire and verify the target identity, establish a valid target track, and confirm ownership transfer before or at the defined target-loss boundary under the tested physical conditions. A communication acknowledgment or commitment without verified acquisition and identity continuity shall not count as a successful handoff.

Component accuracy alone shall not be considered sufficient evidence of success. The evaluation shall connect prediction, TTO estimation, feasibility assessment, negotiation, acquisition, Re-ID, and confirmed ownership transfer.

13. Research-Derived Requirements

The following research-derived requirements (RR) are design drivers extracted from the Literature Review. They define conditions and risks that the system shall explicitly address; they are not, by themselves, claims that the proposed system will outperform every prior method.

  • RR-01: Proactive handoff can preserve continuity better than waiting for loss.
  • RR-02: Visibility loss creates a limited decision window.
  • RR-03: Handoff must fit within available time.
  • RR-04: Appearance-only identity transfer is vulnerable to viewpoint and occlusion changes.
  • RR-05: General negotiation may consume time in a short handoff window.
  • RR-06: Edge computation is constrained.
  • RR-07: Raw video exchange is communication-heavy.
  • RR-08: Simulation may hide physical timing and communication issues.
  • RR-09: Independently moving robots drift in separate odometry frames; transferred target state is only usable in an explicit shared frame with known uncertainty.
  • RR-10: A committed transaction can deadlock if the receiver fails silently or ownership acknowledgements are lost.

16. Constraints and Assumptions

16.1 Constraints

  • SC-01 — Edge Computing Platform: The primary physical implementation shall use an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano as the onboard edge computing platform. Core perception, tracking, prediction, and handoff decision functions shall be designed to operate within its computational and memory constraints.
  • SC-02 — Hardware Platform Sensors and Roles: The physical UGV platform provides RGB/depth vision, LiDAR-based ranging, IMU sensing, and wheel-encoder feedback. RGB visual sensing is the primary modality for target detection, tracking, acquisition, and cross-robot identity verification. IMU and wheel-encoder data shall be available to support ego-motion and robot-state estimation. Depth is permitted to supply metric 3D target position and occlusion/visibility geometry (needed for TTO), and LiDAR supports localization in the shared frame and obstacle/safety awareness; neither shall be an input to the identity (Re-ID) pipeline unless experimentally justified in the Technical Design.
  • SC-03 — Communication: inter-robot coordination shall use lightweight metadata.
  • SC-04 — Moving Cameras: the perception design shall account for UGV/camera motion.
  • SC-05 — Sparse Coverage: the system shall not require continuous overlapping fields of view between adjacent UGVs.
  • SC-06 — Physical Validation: final system claims shall be supported by physical experiments.

16.2 Assumptions

  • The primary validation scenario contains one principal target.
  • One UGV owns the target track at a time, except during an explicitly defined transitional handoff state.
  • At least one other UGV can act as a candidate receiver.
  • The robots have onboard cameras and sufficient edge compute for the selected implementation.
  • A communication path is available during the primary handoff scenario, although delay and packet loss shall be tested as impairments.
  • The initial physical environment is controlled or semi-controlled enough to permit repeatable experiments.

18. Final System Definition

The proposed system is formally defined as:

A decentralized edge-based multi-UGV system that continuously tracks a target locally, predicts impending visibility loss, estimates the remaining time before target unavailability, evaluates whether another UGV can complete the transfer within that time budget, performs lightweight decentralized handoff coordination, enables the receiving UGV to acquire and re-identify the target, and confirms ownership transfer while maintaining identity continuity and operating within edge, communication, and physical-robot constraints.
