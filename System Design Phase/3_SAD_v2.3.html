1. System Components

1.0 Design Principles

The system is decomposed into components according to their responsibilities. This architecture is strictly bound by the hardware constraints and frozen engineering decisions established in SRS v1.3, specifically targeting the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano platform with RGB as the primary modality.

The architecture is governed by the following principles:

PrincipleDescription
Edge-first ComputingPerception, prediction, TTO estimation, decision-making, and Re-ID functions are executable on the UGV's NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano onboard compute.
Component ModularityMajor functions are separated into independently testable and replaceable components. Each participating UGV implements the same logical component model and interface contracts. Component configuration and runtime role may differ between the current owner and receiving UGV.
Identity Separationtarget_id identifies the tracked physical target, while transaction_id identifies the specific handoff attempt. These identifiers shall never be treated as interchangeable.
Freshness & OrderingTime-sensitive messages shall carry timestamp and transaction/state association information sufficient to reject stale or out-of-sequence data.
Ownership ConsistencyOwnership-related messages shall be associated with the current ownership_epoch or equivalent versioning mechanism to prevent stale ownership claims.
Metadata-only CooperationUGVs exchange compact target metadata and control messages rather than continuous raw video.
Deadline AwarenessHandoff negotiation and acquisition operate within the available TTO-based time budget.
Shared Reference FrameAll spatial data exchanged between UGVs is expressed in an explicitly identified shared frame (e.g., a common pre-built map) and carries pose uncertainty. Per-robot odometry frames are never exchanged as if they were aligned.
Acknowledged Ownership & LivenessConfirmation is acknowledged by the previous owner, ownership messages carry a monotonically increasing epoch, and the receiver proves liveness during acquisition. Silent failure must be detectable well before the TTO deadline.
Parameter ProvenanceTime budgets and thresholds are planning values until confirmed by Tier 2 measurements; components read them from C-12 rather than hard-coding them.

1.2 Layer A — Perception and Prediction Layer

C-01 · PerceptionSERVICE

Purpose: Provide local visual perception for target detection and tracking.

Responsibilities:

  • Acquire timestamped RGB camera frames.
  • Produce target detections and localization information required by downstream tracking and prediction functions.
  • Maintain local target tracks.
  • Maintain tracking confidence.
  • Report target visibility and tracking status.
  • Optionally consume depth/LiDAR information when required by the Technical Design for geometric visibility, acquisition, safety, or validation.

Logical Structure:
RGB Camera → Visual Detection → Multi-Object Tracking → Target Track
Depth/LiDAR ──► Optional Geometric / Acquisition / Safety Support
SRS Mapping: FR-01–FR-04, SC-02.

C-02 · EstimationSERVICE

Purpose: Maintain a temporally consistent representation of the target state for downstream prediction and handoff decisions.

Responsibilities:

  • Maintain recent target observations.
  • Estimate target position, velocity, and confidence.
  • Preserve temporal consistency.
  • Provide the latest valid target state to downstream components.

SRS Mapping: FR-05–FR-06.

C-03 · PredictionSERVICE

Purpose: Predict short-term target motion and estimate the Time-to-Occlusion (TTO) with associated uncertainty, representing the remaining time before the target becomes physically occluded or exits the effective FOV.

Responsibilities:

  • Predict short-term target motion with uncertainty.
  • Estimate the applicable visibility-loss condition.
  • Continuously update the TTO estimate.
  • Provide the estimated time budget and uncertainty bounds to the DecisionSERVICE.

SRS Mapping: FR-07–FR-13.

1.3 Layer B — Cooperative Handoff Coordination

C-04 · DecisionSERVICE

Purpose: Evaluate whether handoff criteria are satisfied within the available TTO budget and emit a handoff recommendation.

Responsibilities:

  • Consume the TTO estimate together with its uncertainty/bounds.
  • Consume receiver feasibility information (from C-05) and analytically determine whether the proposed handoff can complete within the available TTO budget and safety margin.
  • Avoid treating an uncertain TTO estimate as an exact deadline.
  • Emit HANDOFF_RECOMMENDED when the configured analytical criteria are satisfied.
  • Reject infeasible handoff attempts.

Boundary: C-04 is an analytical engine only. It does not exert direct kinematic control over the receiver. C-09 is the sole lifecycle authority.
SRS Mapping: FR-14–FR-17.

C-05 · ReceiverSERVICE

Purpose: Assess receiver-side availability and acquisition feasibility using available receiver state information.

Responsibilities:

  • Maintain knowledge of available peer UGVs.
  • Evaluate receiver suitability based on availability, communication capability, kinematic motion constraints, and spatial relationship.
  • In the primary two-UGV scenario, C-05 evaluates the designated Robot B. For future multi-UGV extensions, C-05 may evaluate and rank eligible receiver candidates.

SRS Mapping: FR-18–FR-20.

Two-UGV note: Only one candidate exists; ranked failover (Rank-2) applies only when additional eligible UGVs are present. Eligibility uses a hard resource gate (CPU load, battery, navigation availability) followed by a weighted utility.

C-06 · NegotiationSERVICE

Purpose: Coordinate handoff requests and responses between UGVs through deadline-bounded peer-to-peer communication.

Responsibilities:

  • Exchange handoff requests, responses, target metadata, and control messages.
  • Enforce deadline-bounded communication.
  • Associate messages with the sender, target, transaction_id, and timestamp.
  • Detect communication timeout and failure conditions.

SRS Mapping: FR-21–FR-31, SC-03.

Message set: HANDOFF_REQUEST, HANDOFF_RESPONSE (accept / reject / unavailable), HANDOFF_CANCEL, ACQ_STATUS (liveness), HANDOFF_CONFIRM, HANDOFF_CONFIRM_ACK. All carry sender_id, transaction_id, target_id, ownership_epoch, sequence number and timestamp; spatial payloads carry a frame_id.

C-07 · IdentitySERVICE

Purpose: Verify that the target acquired by the receiving UGV is the same target previously tracked by the sending UGV.

Responsibilities:

  • Maintain a cross-UGV target identity representation.
  • Support multi-cue identity verification using appearance and, where available, spatial, temporal, or predicted-state consistency.
  • Produce an identity match or rejection decision.

SRS Mapping: FR-32–FR-35.

Timing note: The Re-ID engine is kept resident (warm) so that no engine-load time falls inside the handoff budget; the owner maintains a smoothed identity signature of the target at a low rate so one is always ready to send. If Tier 2 shows memory contention, the engine is pre-warmed at HANDOFF_CANDIDATE instead, and the measured load time is added to the budget.

C-08 · AcquisitionSERVICE

Purpose: Enable the receiving UGV to locate and establish a local track for the transferred target within a bounded time.

Responsibilities:

  • Search for and acquire the target using onboard visual perception, with supporting depth/LiDAR information when available and justified.
  • Establish a local track within the acquisition deadline.
  • Report acquisition success or failure.

SRS Mapping: FR-36–FR-38.

C-09 · OwnershipSERVICE

Purpose: Manage the complete handoff transaction lifecycle and control local ownership transfer.

Responsibilities:

  • Maintain the handoff state machine, including explicit recovery branches.
  • Manage negotiation, acquisition, identity-verification, confirmation, and failure/recovery states.
  • Confirm a handoff only after successful acquisition and identity verification.
  • Transfer ownership after confirmation.

Handoff Lifecycle (With Explicit Recovery):

        TRACKING
            ↓
        HANDOFF CANDIDATE
            ↓
        NEGOTIATING
            ↓
        COMMITTED
            ↓
        ACQUISITION
            ↓
        IDENTITY VERIFICATION
            ↓
        CONFIRMED
            │
            ├── FAILURE
            ├── TIMEOUT
            └── INVALID
                    ↓
                RECOVERY
                ↙      ↘
          TRACKING    TERMINAL FAILURE

Ownership Rule: Acceptance of a handoff request (COMMITTED) does not transfer ownership. Ownership changes only after the receiving UGV has acquired and verified the target.
SRS Mapping: FR-39–FR-48.

Consistency rules: (1) The previous owner stays exclusive owner until it receives HANDOFF_CONFIRM and returns HANDOFF_CONFIRM_ACK carrying epoch+1; stale-epoch messages are rejected. (2) During COMMITTED/ACQUISITION/IDENTITY_VERIFICATION, the owner monitors receiver liveness and aborts on loss of liveness (ERR_CANDIDATE_DEAD). (3) On ACK timeout, receiver aborts and owner reclaims target after T_claim, entering STANDBY only when peer reports OWNER role. (4) In the two-UGV baseline an abort enters RECOVERY; re-attempt occurs only if the remaining T_TTO^safe exceeds the budget and another candidate exists.

1.4 Layer C — Platform Observability Support

C-10 · NavigationSERVICE

Purpose: Provide the interface between the cooperative tracking system and the simulated or physical UGV platform.

Responsibilities:

  • Provide robot pose and velocity information derived from IMU and wheel encoders.
  • Abstract the underlying robotics framework.
  • NavigationSERVICE retains final platform-motion and safety authority. Motion requests from AcquisitionSERVICE are advisory/request-based and may be rejected or modified by the navigation/safety layer.

SRS Mapping: FR-49–FR-50, SC-02.

Localization: Publishes robot pose and its covariance in the shared reference frame (map-based localization using LiDAR/IMU/encoders) in addition to the local odometry frame. It also owns the autonomous safe-stop that engages when compute or communication is lost.

C-11 · MonitoringSERVICE

Purpose: Provide observability and record the events required to analyze system behavior and handoff performance.

SRS Mapping: §10, NFR-09–NFR-10.

C-12 · ConfigurationSERVICE

Purpose: Manage runtime parameters and experiment configuration independently from the core system logic.

SRS Mapping: NFR-07–NFR-08.

1.5 Shared Cross-UGV Elements

C-13 · TransportSERVICE

Purpose: Provide the logical communication channel through which C-06 exchanges messages with peer UGVs.

SRS Mapping: Supports FR-21–FR-31.

C-14 · EvaluationSERVICE

Purpose: Aggregate logs from multiple UGVs after an experiment to calculate cross-UGV evaluation metrics.

Boundary Constraint: C-14 is strictly offline and has no authority over online perception, prediction, negotiation, acquisition, or ownership decisions.

SRS Mapping: §11.

2.2 Layer notes

2.2.1 Layer A — Perception and Prediction Layer

C-01 · PerceptionSERVICE

Responsibilities: Acquire timestamped RGB frames. Produce target detections and localization required for tracking. Depth/LiDAR utilized optionally as supporting modalities.

C-03 · PredictionSERVICE

Responsibilities: Include FOV exit boundaries explicitly alongside geometric physical occlusion constraints for TTO calculation. Represent prediction uncertainty.

2.2.2 Layer B — Cooperative Handoff Coordination

C-04 · DecisionSERVICE

Responsibilities: Evaluate handoff timing feasibility against the TTO budget and uncertainty bounds. Process receiver assessments to make analytical handoff recommendations.

C-05 · ReceiverSERVICE

Responsibilities: Assess receiver motion availability and kinematic feasibility before proposing them as viable candidates.

2.2.3 Layer C — Platform Observability Support

C-10 · NavigationSERVICE

Responsibilities: Provide robot pose and velocity. Process advisory motion requests while maintaining absolute control over platform safety and movement execution.

5. Deployment

5.1 Deployment Topology

Primary Validation Topology: Two physical UGVs are used in the core validation scenario: Robot A (current owner) and Robot B (designated receiver).

Architectural Extensibility: The logical receiver-selection architecture permits additional UGVs without changing ownership-transfer semantics.

5.2 Deployment Tiers

TierStatusPurpose
Tier 1 — Simulation TestingSimulation testingValidate the architecture, handoff protocol, and TTO behavior in a controlled environment.
Tier 2 — Jetson Orin Nano Edge BenchmarkingEdge benchmarkingValidate the software on the physical NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano hardware to measure compute and latency feasibility.
Tier 3 — Physical Moving UGVsMandatory primary research deliverableValidate cooperative handoff on physically moving UGVs using the primary RGB-based perception pipeline and supporting robot-state and spatial sensing as justified by the Technical Design.
