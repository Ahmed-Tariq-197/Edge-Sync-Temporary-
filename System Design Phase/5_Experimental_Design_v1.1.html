1. Introduction & Justification

Public tracking datasets such as MOT17 and Market-1501 do not capture the handoff problem: they lack robot-to-robot ownership transfer, deadline-critical timing and edge-hardware constraints, and Market-1501 and MARS use fixed surveillance cameras (some MOT17 sequences do include moving cameras, but none involve cooperative handoff). Simulation alone hides communication delay, thermal throttling and kinematic slippage.

This protocol therefore defines a three-tier evaluation with Tier 3 physical validation on moving UGVs as the mandatory primary evidence (SRS FR-52). Its purpose is to test, not assume, whether integrating proactive TTO prediction, bounded decentralized handoff and identity preservation succeeds under edge constraints.

3. Tier 3 Testbed & Instrumentation

Robots and Network

  • Compute: NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (8 GB) per UGV; power mode and clock settings fixed and recorded (e.g., via nvpmodel/jetson_clocks); temperature and throttling logged (e.g., tegrastats); a 10-minute thermal soak precedes timed runs.
  • Sensors: RGB-D camera (e.g., RealSense D435i/D455) with depth registered to color; RGB and depth FOV and shutter characteristics documented separately; 2D LiDAR for shared-frame localization and obstacle safety; IMU and wheel encoders.
  • Mobility: Mecanum chassis; robots move in every scenario within a stated speed range (default 0.2–0.5 m/s), so camera motion and slippage are part of each trial.
  • Network: 5 GHz Wi-Fi with ROS 2 FastDDS; impairments injected with a traffic-shaping tool (loss, delay, jitter) where specified.
  • Shared frame: both robots localize in a common pre-built map (map frame); localization error is measured against ground truth and reported.

Independent Ground Truth (SRS NFR-13)

  • A time-synchronized external system (e.g., motion capture or a calibrated overhead multi-camera rig with fiducials worn by the target and mounted on robots) provides target pose, both robot poses and event times. Fiducials are used only for ground truth and are never visible to or used by the online system.
  • Occluder geometry is surveyed. True time-to-loss is computed offline from ground-truth poses by the same frustum and ray-intersection definitions as Math v2.3 §1.3 and compared with the online TTO.
  • Ground-truth accuracy and clock synchronization are measured and reported before trials.

4. Baselines, Ablations & Hypotheses

All variants share the same detector, tracker, Re-ID engine and transport so that only the factor under test differs. Hypotheses are stated so they can be falsified; no outcome is assumed in advance.

FactorVariants comparedTestable hypothesis
Handoff timingProposed proactive trigger vs. reactive transfer (request issued only after tracking loss)H1: proactive handoff yields a higher HSR and positive TTO margin than reactive transfer when TTO exceeds the budget.
Prediction modelConstant-velocity Kalman vs. a small learned predictor (e.g., LSTM) at the same horizonH2: the learned model's accuracy gain (ADE/FDE, TTO error) does or does not justify its added latency and memory on the Orin Nano; both outcomes are reported.
NegotiationOne-round request/response vs. multi-round auction/consensus (k = 2, 3 rounds), with and without injected loss/delayH3: extra rounds add measurable latency and reduce HSR under impairment; at no impairment the difference may be small, and this is reported.
Ablations(a) TTO treated as exact (no uncertainty bound); (b) no ego-motion covariance; (c) appearance-only identity vs. multi-cue; (d) no watchdog; (e) no acknowledged confirmationH4: each component improves HSR, identity-transfer success, or deadlock/dual-ownership rate; effect sizes reported.
Proposed systemProactive TTO + one-round request/response + kinematic predictor + multi-cue Re-ID + watchdog + acknowledged confirmationH5: meets the pre-registered criteria of §8 on the physical testbed.

5. Scenarios & Dataset

All scenarios run indoors with both robots moving, in controlled but varied conditions (at least two lighting levels and two target speeds).

Scenario A — Dynamic Occlusion

Setup: the target walks behind a pillar or wall while Robot A tracks. Objective: the TTO estimator predicts the true occlusion within the stated error, and the handoff triggers in Zone 2.

Scenario B — FOV Exit

Setup: the target accelerates perpendicular to A's heading and leaves the frustum before any occlusion. Objective: FOV-exit TTO triggers a proactive handoff.

Scenario C — Sparse Coverage

Setup: A and B have no overlapping views and a blind corridor lies between them. Objective: the metadata payload (3D state in the shared frame, uncertainty, 512-dim signature) is sufficient for B to pre-position and verify identity.

Scenario D — Receiver Failure

Setup: Robot B's compute node is killed (or its link cut) during ACQUISITION; the chassis safe-stop remains active. Objective: A detects loss of liveness and aborts within the watchdog bound, then enters RECOVERY and keeps tracking.

Scenario E — Identity Distractors

Setup: a second person with similar clothing is present near the target at handoff. Objective: measure the cross-robot false-match rate and the effect of multi-cue vs. appearance-only verification.

Scenario F — Network Impairment

Setup: Scenario A repeated with injected loss (0, 5, 10, 20%) and added delay (0, 50, 100, 200 ms). Objective: HSR and latency sensitivity.

Scenario G — Message Faults and Cancellation

Setup: replayed, duplicated and stale-epoch messages are injected; in separate runs the target reverses direction after the request. Objective: zero dual-ownership violations; correct rejection and correct HANDOFF_CANCEL behavior.

Custom Dataset: Mobile UGV Target Handoff Dataset

  • Content: time-synchronized RGB-D frames, IMU, wheel odometry, robot poses, ground-truth target pose, annotations (boxes, identity IDs) and event timestamps.
  • Size: at least 6 subjects with different clothing, with scenario repetitions as in §7.
  • Splits: by subject, never by frame. Any fine-tuning of the detector or Re-ID model uses training subjects only; test subjects are never seen; the pretraining source (e.g., Market-1501) is documented.
  • Annotation: identity labels from ground truth and manual verification; annotator agreement checked on a sample.

6. Metrics & Ground-Truth Definitions

6.1 Event Definitions

  • t_trig: HANDOFF_RECOMMENDED emitted by Robot A.
  • t_confirm: Robot A sends HANDOFF_CONFIRM_ACK (ownership released); Robot B owner on receipt.
  • t_loss: true time of visibility loss for Robot A from ground truth (occlusion or FOV exit).
  • End-to-end latency = t_confirm − t_trig. TTO margin = t_loss − t_confirm; a handoff is timely if the margin is positive.
  • Successful handoff: ownership transferred, B holds a track, B's track is on the correct target (ground-truth identity), and the margin is positive (SRS §12).
  • Feasible trial (evaluator-defined, independent of the system): ground-truth TTO at t_trig ≥ the calibrated total budget and Robot B operational. Results are reported for feasible trials and for all trials, so the system cannot improve its score by declining to act.

7. Sample Size & Statistical Plan

  • Primary scenarios (A, B, C): at least 50 trials each (150 in total), distributed over subjects, speeds and lighting; D: at least 30 trials; E, G: at least 30 trials each; F: at least 20 trials per impairment cell.
  • Why these numbers: with a 95% Wilson interval, 100 pooled feasible trials with at most 4 failures give a lower bound above 90%, and 50 trials with no failures give about 93%. Smaller samples cannot support a "> 90% with confidence" claim.
  • Comparisons: paired design on matched trial conditions; McNemar's test for success/failure, Wilcoxon signed-rank for latency and margins, bootstrap confidence intervals for medians and percentiles; multiple comparisons corrected (Holm).
  • Pre-registration: hypotheses, thresholds and exclusion rules are fixed before Tier 3 data collection. Trials are excluded only for pre-defined faults (e.g., ground-truth dropout) and every exclusion is reported.

8. Success Criteria

System-level verdict (all must hold in Tier 3):
  1. Reliability: pooled Handoff Success Rate on feasible trials ≥ 90%, with the 95% Wilson interval reported (the strong claim requires its lower bound ≥ 90%); the all-trials rate is reported alongside.
  2. Timing: median TTO margin positive, and ≥ 95% of successful handoffs complete within the calibrated total budget (1230 ms planning value unless Tier 2 changes it).
  3. Identity: IDF1 across the handoff within 5 percentage points of a continuous, unobstructed single-camera reference on the same sequences; cross-robot false-match rate reported with its interval.
  4. Consistency and liveness: zero dual-ownership violations; in Scenario D the watchdog aborts within 400 ms in ≥ 95% of trials and the owner keeps tracking.
  5. Edge feasibility: the full stack sustains ≥ 15 FPS with no sustained thermal throttling at the recorded power mode, and memory below 80% of the 8 GB.
  6. Communication: no raw-video exchange; bytes and messages per handoff reported.
The thresholds above are initial values fixed before data collection and may be tightened by Tier 2 evidence, but not relaxed after Tier 3 results are seen.

9. Safety, Ethics & Traceability

  • Safety (NFR-14): hardware emergency stop, speed limits, designated safe zones, and an autonomous chassis stop on loss of compute or link; Scenario D is rehearsed in a dry run before human participants are present; a spotter monitors every trial.
  • Ethics (NFR-15): informed consent and institutional approval for recording people; stated data retention and sharing rules; any released data is de-identified (e.g., face blurring) or restricted.
  • Requirement coverage: FR-14–17 → A, B, G (cancellation); FR-33–35 → C, E; FR-45–47, FR-54–56 → D, F, G; FR-53 → C (shared-frame error); NFR-03, 04, 12 → Tier 2 and all Tier 3 runs; FR-52 → all Tier 3 scenarios.
